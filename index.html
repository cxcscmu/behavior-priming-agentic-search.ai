Beneficial Reasoning Behaviors in Agentic Search and Effective Post-Training to Obtain Them

Identifying and Instilling Critical Reasoning Behaviors for Search Agents

Jiahe Jin, Abhijay Paladugu, Chenyan Xiong

Language Technologies Institute, Carnegie Mellon University

Paper (arXiv) PDF Code

Abstract

Agentic search leverages large language models (LLMs) to solve complex user information needs by executing a multi-step process of planning, searching, and synthesizing information to provide answers. This paradigm introduces unique challenges for LLMs' agentic reasoning capabilities when interacting with retrieval systems and the broader web. In this paper, we propose an LLM-based pipeline to study effective reasoning behavior patterns in agentic search by comparing and analyzing successful and unsuccessful trajectories. Using this pipeline, we identify four beneficial reasoning behaviors: Information Verification, Authority Evaluation, Adaptive Search, and Error Recovery. Based on these findings, we propose a technique called Behavior Priming to train agentic search models. It synthesizes trajectories that exhibit these four behaviors and integrates them into the agentic search model through supervised fine-tuning (SFT), followed by standard reinforcement learning (RL). Experiments on Qwen3-1.7B and Llama3.2-3B-Instruct across three web benchmarks and seven multi-hop QA benchmarks demonstrate that behavior priming yields significant performance gains compared to training models with RL directly and outperforms other SFT-then-RL baselines.

Four Beneficial Reasoning Behaviors

Information Verification

Validating results across multiple sources and cross-checking information to ensure accuracy.

Example: "My task is clear: verify if the quoted text exactly matches Greetham's article. Accuracy is paramount; I'll use 'uncoupled', 'authors', 'mis-transmission', and 'veil' to zero in on the relevant section."

Authority Evaluation

Assessing source reliability and resolving conflicts by weighting reputable sources.

Example: "I'm aiming for the USGS's own reports or databases, like the 'Nonindigenous Aquatic Species' page, to get the most reliable data."

Adaptive Search

Modifying search strategies dynamically based on intermediate results and feedback.

Example: "The search engine may not have indexed the quote perfectly, or the user's quote may differ slightly. I'll refine my strategy."

Error Recovery

Detecting and correcting mistakes through strategic pivots and backtracking.

Example: "My initial search didn't deliver the goods... I need to get more precise."

Key Contributions

  • Automated Behavior Discovery: We design an LLM-based pipeline that automatically identifies beneficial reasoning behaviors by comparing successful and unsuccessful agentic search trajectories.
  • Behavior Priming Method: We propose a post-training technique that systematically instills identified behaviors through supervised fine-tuning (SFT) followed by reinforcement learning (RL).
  • Surprising Finding: Reasoning behaviors matter more than answer correctness — SFT on trajectories with desired behaviors but incorrect answers achieves comparable performance to SFT on correct-answer trajectories after RL.
  • Mechanistic Insights: Behavior priming enhances exploration capabilities (higher pass@k and entropy) and test-time scaling, establishing a robust foundation for effective RL training.

Key Findings:

  • Behavior-primed models achieve over 37% higher overall performance compared to direct RL baselines on web benchmarks (22.3 vs. 13.9 for Qwen3-1.7B; 19.9 vs. 14.5 for Llama3.2-3B-Instruct)
  • Behavior Priming consistently outperforms both random trajectory selection and correctness-focused baselines across all benchmarks
  • The method demonstrates strong generalization across both web agent tasks and multi-hop QA benchmarks

Method Overview

Behavior Priming Pipeline

  1. Trajectory Generation: Generate diverse agentic search trajectories using a powerful model (Gemini-2.5-Flash).
  2. Behavior Identification: Use LLM-based analysis to compare successful vs. failed trajectories and extract beneficial behaviors.
  3. Behavior Mining: Run a reasoner → extractor → merger pipeline to consolidate and quantify behavior patterns.
  4. SFT with Behavior Priming: Fine-tune models on trajectories exhibiting the four identified behaviors.
  5. RL Training: Apply standard reinforcement learning (GRPO) to further improve performance.
